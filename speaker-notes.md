## 1. From Ambition to Action (≈ 0:40)

Hi everyone. Quick context: I'm a student, and I also work as a Junior Software Engineer at Nx iD, usually around 25 to 30 hours a week. Add school, my own programming projects, and some actual rest, and my weeks get crowded fast.

Honestly, I don't always balance it well. So I set three goals for eight weeks, November 1st to December 26th. Those eight boxes are those weeks. None are filled in yet. This is my plan for filling them.

## 2. Why these goals (≈ 0:45)

I didn't want three unrelated goals, because my problem is really one problem: when one part of my week slips, something else pays for it. Usually schoolwork gets pushed to the night before, and that eats my programming time.

So the goals connect. Time management is the base. If I plan my week, I can reserve practice time and put assignments on my calendar the day I get them. And starting early means fewer surprises, so the plan holds.

Long-term, I'd like to lead engineering teams, and you can't manage a team's time if you can't manage your own.

## 3. Three SMART goals (≈ 1:20)

First, the habit. Every Saturday I'll plan the next week in my calendar, with blocks for school, Nx iD, programming and free time. The target is following that schedule at least five days a week. Five, not seven, because some days will go sideways, and I'd rather hit a realistic target for eight weeks.

Second, the skill. At work I mostly build what's assigned. I want 24 hours of focused practice outside that by December 26th, about three hours a week, and I'll use it to build one small project, test it, and document what I learned. Something finished is a better measure than "I studied a bit".

Third, what I avoid: starting assignments. I'm aiming to start at least 80% within 24 hours and submit 90% a day early. Starting can be twenty minutes, just reading the brief and outlining. I'll log when each assignment was given, started and submitted, so the numbers come from data.

## 4. Weekly plan (≈ 1:00)

This is a sample week. My real school and work hours change, so these are realistic estimates.

School runs Sunday to Thursday. After school I have Nx iD blocks, plus a longer Friday morning. That's about 25 hours. If work needs closer to 30, the striped Saturday block covers it, only when needed.

Homework is orange, Sunday to Thursday evenings. Practice is green, Friday afternoon and Saturday morning, because realistically I'm not doing focused programming at ten at night after school and work. The blue block is my Saturday review.

The totals on the right show the week is full, but there's still real time for rest, which is usually the first thing I lose.

## 5. My method (≈ 0:50)

My method is a weekly loop, not a list of tips. I time-block the week, so each goal has a slot instead of fighting for leftover time.

Big assignments get broken into small tasks, like the essay at the bottom. The first task is small, so starting within 24 hours is easy, and the last one lands a day before the deadline.

I track everything: calendar check-offs, practice hours, the assignment log. Every Saturday I compare plan and reality, and adjust.

This suits me because it's how we work at Nx iD: plan a sprint, split work into tickets, track it, run a retro. I'm applying it to my own week.

## 6. Obstacle & solution (≈ 0:50)

The most likely obstacle is something unexpected: an urgent work task, a moved deadline, or a surprise assignment. It will happen.

My response has an order. School deadlines come first. Then I move my practice blocks. Move, not delete. And in my Saturday review, I rebalance the next week.

The chart is an illustration, not real data. Say week three is a crunch and I only get one hour. I add an hour to the next two weeks, and I still reach 24 by December 26th. The weekly number can bend. The eight-week total doesn't.

## 7. Measuring success (≈ 0:40)

So on December 26th, success means: my schedule followed five days a week, 24 practice hours, one documented project, 80% of assignments started within a day, and 90% submitted early.

None of this is done yet. They're targets, and I'll check them every Saturday, starting October 31st, the day before week one.

What I'm really building is reliability. At school, that means less last-minute stress. As an engineer, it's what gets you trusted with bigger work. Thanks for listening.
